Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my time in the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have developed technical skills, strengthened my problem-solving abilities, and gained a better understanding of where I want to take my career. When I originally started the program, my goal was to become a software engineer. However, as I progressed through my coursework and gained more experience working with enterprise networking equipment, my interests began shifting toward network engineering. I still value everything I have learned about programming and software development, but I now see those skills as an advantage in a career that involves designing, maintaining, troubleshooting, and securing computer networks. Completing this capstone has allowed me to reflect on that growth and demonstrate how my education and professional experience complement each other.

One of the most valuable lessons I have learned throughout the program is the importance of collaboration and communication. Computer science professionals rarely work completely independently, especially when supporting systems that other employees, departments, and customers depend on. Through my work as a Spectrum Enterprise technician, I have learned that resolving technical problems requires more than understanding the equipment. It also involves communicating clearly, gathering information, documenting issues, and working with others to reach a solution. My coursework reinforced these skills by requiring me to explain technical decisions, participate in discussions, and present solutions in ways that different audiences could understand. These experiences have helped me recognize how effective communication and collaboration contribute to organizational decision-making.

My networking coursework has been especially valuable in helping me identify my career direction. Through assignments involving routers, switches, network address translation, routing protocols, VLANs, and network security, I gained experience designing and troubleshooting networks. In one Packet Tracer project, I configured separate networks, implemented DHCP services, established routing, and tested connectivity between devices. These exercises helped me understand how individual network components work together and how configuration decisions can affect an entire environment. They also strengthened my ability to approach technical problems systematically rather than immediately assuming what is causing an issue. As I continue developing professionally, I plan to build on these skills by pursuing the Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA) certification and expanding my knowledge of enterprise networking.

The Computer Science program also strengthened my understanding of software engineering, algorithms, and data structures. Learning how to write and organize code taught me the importance of developing solutions that are maintainable, efficient, and reliable. Through different programming assignments, I learned to evaluate how software processes information and how design decisions can affect performance. These skills are useful beyond traditional software development because modern networking increasingly involves automation, software-defined infrastructure, and systems that depend on applications and APIs. Understanding how software works gives me an additional perspective when troubleshooting problems that may involve both network connectivity and application behavior.

Database development has been another important part of my education. Working with MongoDB and other database concepts helped me understand how information is structured, validated, stored, and retrieved. I learned that database reliability depends on more than successfully saving information. Appropriate data types, validation rules, indexing, and efficient queries all contribute to the performance and integrity of an application. These concepts are also relevant to enterprise technology because organizations rely on accurate information for monitoring, reporting, troubleshooting, and decision-making.

Security has become one of the most important considerations in how I approach technology. My coursework taught me to think about potential vulnerabilities rather than focusing only on whether a program or network functions correctly. I gained experience with authentication, access controls, input validation, and network security principles. I also learned that protecting systems requires a combination of secure design, appropriate configuration, and continued monitoring. My goal is to carry this security mindset into network engineering, where protecting organizational resources and maintaining reliable access are essential responsibilities.

Another experience that contributed to my growth was working with a Raspberry Pi to develop a smart thermostat. This project required me to connect hardware components, read temperature data from a sensor, use buttons to adjust settings, control LEDs, and display information on an LCD screen. I also implemented a state machine to control the system's behavior. Troubleshooting the wiring and software helped me develop patience and a more methodical approach to identifying problems. It was particularly valuable because it connected programming concepts with physical hardware and showed me how software interacts with devices in real-world systems.

My CS-499 capstone brings these different areas of computer science together through the Travlr Getaways application, which I originally developed in CS-465 Full Stack Development I. I selected this application because it provided an opportunity to demonstrate growth in software design and engineering, algorithms and data structures, and databases. Rather than selecting three unrelated projects, I chose to improve one full-stack application across all three categories. This allowed each enhancement to build on the previous work and demonstrated how changes in one part of a system can affect other components.

The first enhancement focused on software design and engineering. I improved authentication, input validation, error handling, and security-related functionality. The second enhancement focused on algorithms and data structures by implementing server-side searching, filtering, sorting, and pagination. These changes improved how trip information could be retrieved and displayed. The third enhancement focused on databases by strengthening the Mongoose schema, improving data validation, introducing appropriate numeric data types, and adding database indexes and timestamps. Together, these enhancements demonstrate my ability to evaluate an existing application, identify opportunities for improvement, implement changes, and test the results.

Completing these enhancements also reinforced the importance of reviewing my own work and responding to feedback. My instructor's comments throughout the capstone helped me understand the value of documenting code and presenting technical work clearly. All three enhancements received full credit, which gave me confidence in the progress I had made while reminding me that professional development does not end when a project is submitted. There is always an opportunity to improve documentation, maintainability, security, and the overall experience for someone reviewing or using the application.

As I prepare to complete my bachelor's degree, I feel that the Computer Science program has provided me with a strong technical foundation and a clearer professional direction. My goal is to continue advancing in network engineering while using my background in software development, databases, and cybersecurity to become a more versatile IT professional. I want to be someone who can understand how systems work together, communicate effectively with different teams, and contribute to solutions that are secure, reliable, and valuable to an organization. This ePortfolio represents both the technical skills I have developed and my commitment to continuing to learn as technology evolves.
